The Keeper's Light

Chapter One · The Point

The Keeper and the Lens-Maker

At the end of the harbour town, on a finger of black rock called the Point, stood a striped lighthouse. Its keeper was Kayode, who could row faster than any fisherman and climb all hundred and twelve steps without stopping for breath.

His wife, Amara, made the lens. She ground glass by hand into rings that caught the smallest flame and threw it miles out to sea. Every night the fishing fleet sailed out by that light, and every morning it brought them home.

Chapter Two · The storm

The Night She Was Born

Their daughter came early, on the wildest night anyone could remember. Rain hammered the shutters of the keeper's cottage and the wind made the tower hum like a struck bell. Amara named her Ifeoluwa, and they called her Ife.

But Kayode could not stop looking out to sea. The whole fleet was still out there, forty boats with half the town aboard, and somewhere beyond the reef they were steering by his light.

Chapter Three · Midnight

The Light Goes Out

Just after midnight a wave taller than the tower struck the Point. It burst the lantern windows, drowned the flame, and smashed Amara's lens into a thousand pieces across the stones.

Kayode climbed through the spray to the top. Only the heart of the lens had survived, a disc of glass no bigger than a hand. Without the light, the fleet would run onto the reef in the dark, and the surge behind them would reach the town before morning.

Chapter Four · Before dawn

The Choice

Beyond the reef stood the Outer Rock, where the first keepers had burned a fire long before the tower was built. A beacon lit there would show the fleet the one safe channel. But whoever lit it would be standing in the path of the surge when it came.

Amara would not let him go alone. The heart of the lens needed a steady hand while the fire was fed. So they wrapped Ife in her mother's red shawl and carried her through the rain to Mama Yemi, the old harbourmaster.

Kayode hung his brass keeper's key around the baby's neck. "Keep it until you need it," he said. Then they walked down to the boat together and did not look back, because they knew that if they did, they could not go.

Chapter Five · The beacon

The Outer Rock

They rowed through waves that stood up like walls, and somehow they reached the Outer Rock. In a hollow at its top lay the old beacon bowl. Kayode broke up the boat's own seats for fuel and soaked them in lamp oil, and Amara raised the heart of the lens behind the flame.

Far out in the dark, forty boats were running straight for the reef.

Chapter Six · Dawn

The Wave

All night they kept the fire alive, and boat after boat slipped through the channel into the harbour. When the grey light came, only one boat was still outside the reef: old Bayo's, slow and heavy with water.

Then the surge arrived, a wall of sea that filled the whole horizon. The fishermen on the quay watched the beacon rise with it, still burning, still pointing the way, until Bayo's boat crossed the harbour mouth. Then the wave passed over the Outer Rock, and the rock was bare.

Chapter Seven · Ten years on

The Keeper's Girl

Mama Yemi raised Ife in the harbourmaster's house. The tower stood dark, because no one in town could grind a lens like Amara's, and no one wanted the keeper's job after what had happened.

And people told the story wrong. Some had seen the keepers rowing away from the Point in the storm, and they whispered that Kayode and Amara had abandoned the light and fled. Ife heard the whispers. Every evening she climbed the dark tower with the brass key around her neck and sat in the empty lantern room, looking out at the Outer Rock.

Chapter Eight · The second storm

The Heart of the Lens

When Ife was sixteen, a storm like the old one came down on the town with the fleet still at sea. Mama Yemi took a box from under her bed. Inside, wrapped in a faded red shawl, lay a disc of glass. "It washed up at the foot of the Outer Rock the morning after," she said. "Bayo found it. It was still warm."

Then she told Ife everything: the shattered lantern, the fire on the rock, forty boats brought home. Her parents had not run from their post. They had carried it out to sea.

Ife ran to the Point, unlocked the tower with her father's key, and set the heart of the lens behind a new flame. For the first time in sixteen years, the light came on. One by one, the boats turned for home.

Afterword · The night of lanterns

What the Light Keeps

Ife became the keeper of the Point. On the anniversary of the great storm, the whole town carries paper lanterns down to the quay and sets them on the water, one for every boat that came home, and two more that drift out toward the Outer Rock.
